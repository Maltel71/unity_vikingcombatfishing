## Long description
Paste into Steamworks → Store Presence → Description → About This Game. Steam uses BBCode, so the tags below are correct as written.

```
[h2]ABOUT THE GAME[/h2]
Step into the boots of Ragnar, a Viking with two problems: he is hungry, and the woods are full of gnomes.

Ragnar Goes Fishing is a 2D arcade game that puts a fishing rod in one hand and an axe in the other. Every fish you land keeps you alive. Every gnome you cut down buys you time to cast again. Stand your ground long enough and your name goes up in the Halls of Valhalla.

[h2]FISH TO SURVIVE[/h2]
Health does not regenerate. Fish is the only thing standing between Ragnar and the afterlife.

Cast into the lake and wait for the bite, then reel it in before it shakes loose. Perch, roach, flounder, pike, salmon and rainbow trout each restore a different amount of health and are worth different points. The rarer the catch, the harder the fight to land it.

Leave a fish lying on the bank too long and it turns. Flies find it, and eating it costs you health instead of restoring it. Sometimes the lake gives you an old boot, which is worth nothing at all — but it does make Ragnar furious.

[h2]BATTLE NORDIC FOLKLORE[/h2]
You will not fish in peace. Gnomes come in waves, more of them each time, and they do not care that you are mid-cast.

Every few waves something worse arrives:

[list]
[*][b]The Troll[/b] — slow, enormous, and hits like a falling tree
[*][b]The Gnome Berserker[/b] — fast, relentless, and swings before you can back off
[*][b]Nacken[/b] — a drowned hand that rises from the lake and reaches for you, with gnomes sprinting in while you deal with it
[/list]

Beat them and they come back stronger. There is no last wave.

[h2]BUILD THE RAGE[/h2]
Every clean hit fills the rage meter. Take a hit and you lose some of it. Fill it completely and unleash the berserk swing — three times the damage, wide enough to clear a crowd, and enough to send a troll staggering backwards.

[h2]KEY FEATURES[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Two jobs at once[/b] — reel with one hand, fight with the other, and learn when to drop the rod
[*][b]Fish that fight back[/b] — six species with their own bite times, reel difficulty and rewards
[*][b]Endless waves[/b] — the gnomes keep coming and keep multiplying
[*][b]Three bosses from Nordic folklore[/b] — each returning stronger every time you kill it
[*][b]The berserk swing[/b] — earned through clean fighting, spent in one devastating blow
[*][b]Halls of Valhalla[/b] — a global leaderboard where your catch and your kill count decide your place
[*][b]10 achievements[/b] — from your first fish to clearing 250 gnomes
[/list]

[h2][/h2]
Fish. Fight. Die well.
```

## What was wrong in the old text

- **The game was called "Ragnar Goes To Valhalla".** The build is called Ragnar Goes Fishing. The two must match.
- **"heavy-hitting Trolls"** — there is one Troll, not several, and the Gnome Berserker was missing entirely.
- **"native Swedish fish species"** — rainbow trout was introduced to Sweden from North America and flounder is a sea fish. "Native" invites corrections in the reviews. Changed to plain descriptions.
- **The berserk attack was not mentioned at all**, even though the rage meter is the deepest mechanic in the game.
- **Rotting fish and the old boot were missing** — both are memorable and worth selling.
- **"Reclaim your place in Valhalla"** — reclaim implies Ragnar had it and lost it. Changed to earn.
- **"Swedish fishing mechanics"** — fishing mechanics have no nationality.
- **Bosses returning at higher levels was not mentioned**, which is what makes the game endless rather than a short loop.

## Note on the name

Steam will not let you rename the store page freely once it is published, and the executable, the window title and the store page should agree. Decide now:

- Keep **Ragnar Goes Fishing** — matches the build, plain and descriptive, reads like an arcade game.
- Switch to **Ragnar Goes to Valhalla** — grander, but then change Product Name in Player Settings and rebuild.

The copy above uses Ragnar Goes Fishing. Swapping it is one find-and-replace if you choose the other.

## Umlauts

The copy writes Nacken without the diaeresis, because Steam store pages handle it fine but some storefront widgets and the Steam client search do not. If you prefer Näcken, write it that way everywhere — including the achievement descriptions — so it is consistent.
